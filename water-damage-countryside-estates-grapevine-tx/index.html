First-hour safety: what to do while you wait

Do only what is safe.

  1. Stay out of water that reaches outlets, appliances or the panel, and call 911 or the electric utility for any electrical or gas concern.
  2. Close the shut-off valve at the failed fixture, or the main valve, only if you can reach it dry.
  3. Do not run a household vacuum or fans through standing water.
  4. Take photos and short video of the damage before moving items.
  5. Move small belongings and rugs to dry ground and keep pets away from the water.

Spotting a leak early

According to the City of Grapevine's utility billing FAQ, meters are read every month and reads are never estimated, and a meter that is wearing out tends to under-read. A bill that rises without a change in how you live is worth investigating for a hidden leak, even one inside a wall.

Other signs are a damp baseboard, a musty smell in a closet and a ceiling stain that grows. Photograph it and call, because water inside a wall keeps spreading even when the surface looks dry.

Water heaters and the permit question

The City of Grapevine's building services FAQ states that a permit is required to replace a water heater and that it is illegal for anyone, homeowners included, to replace one without it. The city's turnaround goal for simple trade permits such as water heaters is the same day.

If a tank has failed, we handle the water and the drying, and a plumber replaces the heater. Our water heater leak cleanup page explains the drying side, and kitchen water damage covers sink and dishwasher leaks.

Extraction, drying and what comes out

We pull standing water with extraction units, map moisture with meters and a thermal imaging camera, and set air movers and dehumidifiers across the wet area. Readings are repeated each visit so equipment leaves only when the materials test dry.

Saturated carpet pad, wet insulation and drywall that has gone soft are commonly removed. After drying, water damage repair replaces what was taken out, and emergency water removal is the page for water that is still spreading.
