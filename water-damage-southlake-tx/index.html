Southlake homes from the boomburb decades

Southlake stayed rural until Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport opened in the 1970s, then grew quickly as a boomburb through the 1980s, 1990s and 2000s. Its population rose from 1,023 in 1960 to 21,519 in 2000 and 31,265 in the 2020 census. Most of its homes therefore share a few construction decades, and their original systems wear out on a similar timeline.

That matters for water damage. Water heaters, supply lines, roof flashing and condensate drains installed during those building years are now at or past typical service life. A leak that starts in an upstairs bath can travel through a ceiling into a kitchen before anyone sees a stain.

What to do first if water is still spreading in Southlake

Power and wet areas

Cut breakers only if you can get to the panel on dry ground. If outlets or fixtures are wet, stay out and call the electric utility or 911.

Near the lake: a flood question to ask

Southlake takes its name from Grapevine Lake, built on Denton Creek between 1948 and 1952 for flood control. The lake's spillway was used in June 2015 for the first time since 1981. Standard home policies exclude flood, so ask your insurer about flood coverage and look up your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, especially if your home is near low ground.

For flood damage restoration after storm water or a sewer backup, treat the water as contaminated and stay out until a crew has made the area safe.
