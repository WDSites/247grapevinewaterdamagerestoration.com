Summer, freeze and the water problems Euless sees

Euless sits in Tarrant County, 16 square miles between Fort Worth and Dallas, and the southwestern part of Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport lies inside its limits. What to do while you wait if water is still spreading

Do only what is safe.

Close the shutoff

Fixture valve first, then the main. For irrigation, close the backflow valve.

Power safety

Cut breakers for wet areas only if the panel is dry and reachable. Wet outlets or fixtures mean stay out and call the electric utility or 911.

Lift valuables

Raise electronics and paper. Do not strain to move furniture.

Protect people

Keep children and pets out of any water that came from outdoors or a drain.

Photograph

Document each wet room before moving anything.

Call dispatch

We will ask where the water entered and what it touched.

Euless water supply and why it matters

The City of Euless states that its primary water comes from the Trinity River Authority, drawn from Cedar Creek and Richland Chambers reservoirs in East Texas, pumped to Lake Arlington and treated before delivery to Euless, Bedford, Colleyville, Grapevine and North Richland Hills. The city also runs its own wells in the Trinity Aquifer as a secondary source.

Euless Public Works' Water and Wastewater division maintains the city's lines and answers water questions at 817-685-1580. If water is surfacing in the yard or the street, that division handles it. If it is inside your home, we do.

Drought stages, irrigation and hidden leaks

Euless ties lawn watering limits to the combined capacity of Tarrant Regional Water District reservoirs. Under Stage One, below 75 percent, watering is limited to two days a week, and tighter stages follow below 60 and 45 percent.

Those schedules push many homeowners to run irrigation on timers. A cracked line or a stuck valve can run unnoticed for weeks beside a foundation, and water that reaches a slab edge or a crawl space becomes a restoration job. A sudden jump in the water bill is the first clue.
