What we do

247 Grapevine Water Damage Restoration answers the emergency line for water damage restoration and mold remediation in Grapevine, Colleyville, Southlake, Coppell, Euless and Flower Mound. A dispatcher asks what happened, how far the water has spread and what is safe to do while help is arranged, then sends licensed and insured restoration professionals.

Our office address is 2555 SW Grapevine Pkwy, Grapevine, TX 76051. The line is open 24 hours a day, every day, including holidays.

How we work

We start with safety, then the source of the water, then the extent of the damage. Scope and price are explained before work starts. Drying plans follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration, and mold work follows ANSI/IICRC S520. These are published industry standards, and you are welcome to ask any restoration company whether its crews work to them.

In practice that means moisture is measured before and after drying, wet materials are sorted by water category and mold work is contained so it does not spread to clean rooms. We explain which of those steps apply to your job when we explain scope and price.

How a call works

Every call follows the same path, whether it is 2 p.m. or 2 a.m.

  1. A dispatcher answers and asks where the water is coming from and whether it is still running.
  2. You get safety guidance first, including how to shut off the water and when to stay out of a room.
  3. A crew is arranged, with same-day response offered when availability allows.
  4. On site, the crew finds the source, reads moisture and explains scope and price before work starts.
  5. Drying, cleanup, mold work or rebuild follows, with photos and readings kept for your records.

The standards behind the work

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for professional water damage restoration. It sorts water into three categories by contamination and describes how drying is planned and checked. ANSI/IICRC S520 is the matching standard for professional mold remediation, covering containment, removal and verification.

Standards describe good practice, and they do not certify any one company. That is why we suggest asking every restoration company how its crews apply them, and why we say so plainly instead of leaning on logos.

What to ask any restoration company before you sign

These questions apply to us and to anyone else you call.

  • Is the company licensed and insured, and can you see proof of insurance?
  • For mold work, what is the TDLR license number, and does it check out with the state?
  • Will scope and price be explained in writing before work starts?
  • How will drying be checked, and will you get the moisture readings?
  • Who handles contents, and what happens to items that cannot be saved?
  • Who will you speak to if something changes mid-job?

A company that is comfortable with these questions is usually comfortable with the work.

What we do not claim

We do not promise an arrival time, we do not promise how an insurance claim will be decided and we do not offer equipment rentals. Water damage restoration is not waterproofing, and we say so when a foundation or drainage contractor is the right call. We list no individual names, ratings or reviews on this site because we do not publish what we cannot verify.

If something on this site looks wrong, such as a ZIP code, a phone number or a local detail, tell us at contact@247grapevinewaterdamagerestoration.com and we will check it against its source.

Why the line is call-only

Water damage moves faster than an email thread. A form asks you to wait, and a phone call gets a person who can tell you to shut off the main valve in the first minute. That is why every button on this site is a phone number, and why we have no contact form, chat box or booking widget.

It also keeps the process honest. Scope and price are explained to you in a conversation before work starts, and you can ask any question while the dispatcher is still on the line.

Call 911 or the utility first when

Water is touching outlets, the electrical panel or light fixtures, you smell gas, a ceiling is sagging with water, or sewage is flooding the home. Call 911 or the electric or gas utility for those risks, then call us.
