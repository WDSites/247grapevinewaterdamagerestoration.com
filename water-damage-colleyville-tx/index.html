Problems we see in a fast-growing north Tarrant suburb

Colleyville began as a small farm town and grew from 1,491 residents in 1960 to 26,057 in the 2020 census, a rise often tied to its position near Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport. Fast growth means a large stock of homes built in a limited number of decades, and those homes age together. Water heaters, braided supply lines, dishwasher hoses and roof flashing tend to wear out around the same time.

The practical result is that most calls begin with an appliance or a supply line, not a river. A water heater tank that splits in a hallway closet or a washer valve that gives out in a laundry room can spread water into hardwood, carpet and drywall in a few hours. Shut off the supply valve at the appliance or at the main and call us.

Before we arrive: if water is still spreading

Take these steps only if it is safe to do so.

Stop the source

Close the valve at the fixture, appliance or main water line. If you cannot find it, call and we will help you locate it.

Cut power only if safe

Turn off breakers for wet areas only if you can reach the panel on dry ground. Wet outlets or a wet panel mean stay out and call the electric utility or 911.

Lift what you can

Move valuables, electronics and paper off wet floors. Leave heavy furniture where it is.

Keep people clear

Keep children and pets out of the water, especially if it came from a toilet, a drain or the outdoors.

Photograph first

Take photos and video of the damage before you move or discard anything. Your insurer will ask for them.

Insurance basics for Colleyville homeowners

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a failed supply line, is often covered by homeowner policies, and gradual leaks and flooding often are not. Flood coverage is separate.
