How a frozen pipe turns into a flooded room in Grapevine

Frozen pipe water damage in Grapevine, TX begins when water inside a supply line turns to ice and expands. The pipe wall splits, often along a seam or at a fitting, and the ice plug keeps the opening sealed until temperatures rise.

Lines in attics, inside exterior walls, in garages and on outdoor spigot runs sit closest to the cold, so they split first. Once the thaw starts, water can run inside a wall or above a ceiling for hours before a stain appears.

A frozen pipe that has not burst is still a warning, because the ice has already stressed the line and it can fail on the next cold night.

Safety in the first hour of a frozen or split pipe

Order matters here, because thawing before the valve is closed is how a small split becomes a soaked floor.

  1. Close the main water valve before you thaw anything, so a cracked pipe has nothing to release as the ice melts.
  2. If water is touching outlets, a light fixture or the panel, stay out and call the electric utility or 911 before you call us. Cut power only from a dry panel you can reach safely.
  3. Thaw slowly with a hair dryer or warm towels while a faucet stays open, never with a torch or open flame.
  4. Move rugs, boxes and electronics off wet floors and out from under wet ceilings.
  5. Photograph the frozen section, the split and every wet surface before anything is moved.

How we pull the water and dry the structure after a thaw

Crews follow the published ANSI/IICRC S500 reference standard for water damage work, and the details change with the water category and the materials involved.

  1. Confirm the water is off and note where the split is, since a plumber repairs the pipe and we do not.
  2. Extract standing water with portable extraction units.
  3. Scan walls, ceilings and floors with moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera, paying close attention to exterior walls and attic runs.
  4. Remove materials that cannot dry in place, usually saturated insulation, soft drywall and carpet pad.
  5. Place air movers and LGR dehumidifiers, add drying mats on hard floors, and seal the area when airflow needs help.
  6. Re-read moisture on each visit and pull equipment only when readings match dry reference levels.

Insurance questions after a burst pipe

Many policies treat a pipe that splits from a freeze as sudden damage, but wording differs, so check your policy and ask your insurer how freeze losses are handled. Gradual leaks and seepage are often excluded, which is why a slow drip you ignored for weeks gets different treatment than a split you caught the day it thawed.

Report the loss early and keep the damaged section of pipe if you can. We do not promise how a claim will be decided.

Thaw-day mistakes that make the damage worse

The most common error is opening the heat on a frozen line before closing the main valve. A second is aiming a heater or torch at a pipe, which can start a fire or scorch framing.

People also forget the attic and garage. Water that drained quietly into insulation can sit there for days, so check ceilings below attic lines even if the kitchen looks dry. Call a plumber early, because the pipe needs repair before drying can finish.
