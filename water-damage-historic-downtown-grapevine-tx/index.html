Water damage near Historic Downtown Grapevine

Historic Downtown Grapevine is the center of the city, around Main Street and City Hall at 200 S. Main Street. A local relocation guide describes the nearby residential streets as a mix of older homes and newer infill, from mid-century ranches to newer Victorian-inspired houses.

We do not assume any particular house has plaster walls, old wiring or original flooring. If yours does, tell the dispatcher, because those materials change how water is extracted and how gently drying has to be done.

The city's historic districts

The City of Grapevine's historic preservation page says the city has the Historic Township District and five local historic districts. Exterior alterations inside them must follow design guidelines through a Certificate of Appropriateness from the Historic Preservation Commission.

The city adopted its Design Guidelines for Historic Commercial and Residential Properties on November 19, 2003. If storm or leak damage means exterior repairs, ask the commission whether your property and your planned work need approval before anyone starts.

First-hour safety steps

Keep each step safe.

  • Stay out of rooms where water reaches outlets, old fixtures or the panel, and call 911 or the electric utility for any electrical or gas risk.
  • Shut off the water at the nearest dry valve, or at the main if you can reach it safely.
  • Do not pry up wet flooring or pull at wet plaster, because you can damage material that could be dried.
  • Photograph the damage, the source and any finishes you want to keep.
  • Lift small valuables and rugs off the floor and leave heavy furniture alone.

Drying older materials with care

Extraction units remove standing water, and moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera show where moisture has gone. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers then dry the structure, with readings repeated at each visit.

Solid wood and plaster can hold moisture longer and may react to fast drying, so readings guide the pace. Crews follow ANSI/IICRC S500, the published reference standard for water damage restoration, and record what was dried, what was removed and why.

Permits and who to ask

The Building Inspection Department is at 200 S. Main Street and takes one combined permit application for plumbing, electrical and mechanical work. Rules vary by job, so call the department about your repair. A plumber handles pipe repairs, and we handle the water and the drying.

For appliance and sink leaks, see appliance leak water damage and kitchen water damage. For water that is still spreading, emergency water removal is the first page to read.

Flood maps and insurance

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. The FEMA Flood Map Service Center lets you search a specific address, and a conversation with your insurer will tell you whether you hold flood coverage, which standard homeowners policies leave out.

Texas Department of Insurance guidance says most policies cover sudden and accidental water damage and exclude gradual leaks. Check your policy. We keep dated photos, readings and an itemized scope on file, and a coverage decision stays with your insurer.

Rebuilding after the structure is dry

Once readings are normal, water damage repair replaces drywall, flooring and paint that could not be saved. In a historic district, finishes and exterior changes may need to match city guidelines, so settle those questions before ordering materials.
