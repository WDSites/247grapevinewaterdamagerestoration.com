Emergency Water Removal in Grapevine, TX

Emergency water removal pulls standing water out of a home with pumps and extraction units, so floors, walls and contents stop soaking up more of it.

When standing water is an emergency

Standing water in a Grapevine home is an emergency from the first minute. Every hour lets it soak further into carpet, drywall and subfloor. Our line is answered 24/7, and same-day response is offered when crew availability allows. No arrival time is promised.

Before the crew arrives, shut off the main water valve if the water is coming from a supply line, and turn off power to the wet area only if you can reach the panel with dry feet. If water is touching outlets or the panel, stay out and call the electric utility or 911 before you call us. Lift valuables, keep people and pets out of the water and do not run a household vacuum over it.

What water extraction involves

Extraction removes bulk water fast, before drying starts, in line with the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard. Crews use submersible pumps for deep water, portable extraction units for carpeted rooms and truck-mounted units when the volume is large. Weighted extraction tools press water out of carpet and pad, and pad is usually pulled because it holds water against the subfloor.

After the bulk water is gone, the job moves into drying. Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera show where water traveled, and water mitigation places air movers and dehumidifiers. Extraction alone is not enough, because a surface that feels dry can still hold moisture inside the wall.

Burst pipe water damage

A burst pipe floods fast. Supply lines run at household pressure, so a split pipe can put hundreds of gallons into a house in an hour. Shut off the main valve first and open a faucet on the lowest level to drain remaining pressure if you can do it safely.

In February 2021 Grapevine's mayor signed a disaster declaration after the winter storm, and many homes dealt with pipes that split after a freeze. Frozen lines often fail when they thaw, so a leak that starts after a cold snap may show up hours later inside a wall. A crew traces the wet area with a thermal camera and opens only what the readings point to.

Photograph the damage, keep the broken section of pipe if you can and call your plumber to repair the line. Water removal handles the water. A plumber fixes the pipe.

Common sources of standing water

These are the situations behind most emergency removal calls.

Supply line or fitting failure

Braided hoses, ice maker lines and shutoff valves under sinks fail without warning and run until someone shuts the valve.

Water heater leak

Tanks corrode and split, and a leaking one can empty its tank onto the floor of a closet or garage.

Appliance overflow

A dishwasher, washing machine or refrigerator line can discharge gray water across a kitchen or laundry room.

Roof or window leak

Storm water through a roof fixture or window frame soaks insulation and ceilings before it reaches the floor.

Crawl space and whole-house flooding

Water in a crawl space is easy to miss because nobody looks there. A broken irrigation backflow device, a plumbing leak under the house or storm runoff can leave standing water against the subfloor and insulation. Crews pump the space, remove wet insulation and dry the underside of the floor.

When the whole house floods, extraction is staged room by room, starting where the water is deepest and where the electrical risk is lowest. For storm or lake water, the work shifts to flood damage restoration because that water is treated as contaminated.

Cost factors and insurance

Removal pricing depends on the amount and depth of water, the water category, how many rooms are affected, the materials that must come out and how long drying runs afterward. Scope and price are explained before work starts. Want your real number? Describe the situation when you call.
