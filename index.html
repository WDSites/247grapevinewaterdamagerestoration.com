What water damage restoration covers

Water damage restoration is the work of stopping water, removing it, drying the structure and repairing the materials it harmed. In a Grapevine home that can mean a failed supply line behind a dishwasher, a roof leak after a storm, a clogged air conditioner drain line or a sewer backup through a floor drain.

Each job starts the same way. A crew finds the source, measures how far the moisture traveled with moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera, and sets a drying plan that follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration. The plan decides which materials can be dried in place and which must come out.

Speed matters because water keeps moving. Drywall wicks moisture upward, carpet pad holds it against the subfloor and wet framing can stay damp for days. That is why emergency water removal and water mitigation come first, and rebuilding comes after the structure tests dry.

Which water category are you dealing with?

The S500 standard sorts water into three categories by how contaminated it is. The category changes what can be saved, what protective gear crews wear and how the cleanup is handled.

CategoryTypical sourceWhat it meansHow crews handle it
Category 1Broken supply line, tub overflow, rain through a roof leakClean at the source but it degrades as it sitsExtract, dry in place, keep most materials
Category 2Washing machine or dishwasher discharge, toilet overflow with urine onlyContains contaminants that can cause discomfortExtract, remove wet carpet pad and porous materials, treat surfaces
Category 3Sewage backup, creek or lake flood waterGrossly contaminated, treat as unsafeContainment, removal of porous materials, sanitizing, strict safety steps

Not sure which category you have? Describe what you see when you call and the dispatcher will tell you what to do while you wait. Any sewage or flood water is treated as Category 3 until a crew rules it out.

What to do in the first hour before help arrives

Water damage gets worse by the hour, so the first sixty minutes belong to you. These steps lower the damage and keep people safe while a crew is on the way.

  1. Stop the source. Shut off the main water valve, or the local valve under a sink or behind a toilet or washer.
  2. Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water. If outlets, ceiling fixtures or the panel are wet, stay out and call the electric utility or 911 first.
  3. Lift valuables, electronics and paper off wet floors, and move wet rugs to a dry spot.
  4. Photograph and video everything before you move or discard it. Your insurer will ask for this.
  5. Keep children and pets away from standing water, and treat anything from a sewer or lake as contaminated.
  6. Open windows only if the weather helps. Do not use a household vacuum on standing water.

If a ceiling is bulging with water, do not stand under it. A small hole made by a pro lets the water drain in a controlled way, and a collapse can injure you and ruin the room.

Where water damage comes from in Grapevine

Grapevine sits beside Grapevine Lake, a flood control reservoir built on Denton Creek in the early 1950s. After storms in June 2015 the spillway was used for the first time since 1981, and FM 2499 closed for an extended stretch while nearby cities declared emergencies.

Homes near the shoreline or along Tah-Wah-Karro Creek should check the FEMA flood map for their address and ask an insurer about flood coverage, because standard home policies exclude flood.

Winter is the other local trigger. Grapevine's mayor signed a disaster declaration on February 16, 2021 after the winter storm, and the city waived permit fees for plumbing repairs afterward. Frozen supply lines that split inside walls can run for hours before anyone notices, so a freeze warning is a good moment to know where your main shutoff is.

The rest is ordinary household failure. Washing machine hoses, water heater tanks, refrigerator ice maker lines and air conditioner condensate lines cause most of the leaks that reach us in Texas homes. A line that drips for weeks behind a wall looks small until the drywall softens.

Insurance claims and water damage

Most homeowner and renters policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe or a failed washer hose. Gradual leaks and neglect are often excluded, and flood damage usually needs a separate flood policy. Check your policy or ask your insurer what applies to your loss.

The Texas Department of Insurance tells policyholders to report damage as soon as possible, photograph or video it before repairs, keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them and make only temporary repairs until the insurer has looked. We take photos and moisture readings and write a scope you can hand to your adjuster. We do not promise how a claim will be decided.

Mold after water damage, and what restoration is not

Mold needs moisture, so a wall that stayed wet after a leak is where it tends to show up. If you already see mold or smell a musty odor, the mold remediation page explains how containment and removal work and why the leak has to be fixed first. Questions about health effects belong with a physician, and Texas licenses mold assessment and remediation, so ask any company for its TDLR license number.

Water damage restoration is also not waterproofing. If water enters a basement, crawl space or garage because of grading, a foundation crack or a drainage problem, we can dry and restore what it damaged, but a foundation or drainage contractor fixes the cause.

Large losses, vacant homes and whole-house flooding follow the same path with more equipment and more documentation. Callers dealing with a big loss usually need the scope in writing first, and we provide it before work starts.
