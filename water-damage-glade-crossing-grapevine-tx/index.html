A stain, a smell or a soft wall: what it can mean

Water often shows up far from where it started. A brown ring on a ceiling can come from a supply line, a drain or a roof leak above it. A baseboard that lifts, paint that bubbles or drywall that feels soft to the touch points to moisture inside the wall.

None of these signs tells you the cause by itself. A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera show how far the wet area runs, and a plumber or roofer finds and fixes the source. We dry what the water reached.

First-hour safety steps

Keep every step safe.

  • Stay away from any wet area near outlets, light fixtures or the panel, and call 911 or the electric utility if there is a shock, spark or burning smell.
  • Do not stand under a ceiling that sags or bulges, because wet drywall can fall.
  • Shut off the water at the nearest valve if it is dry and reachable.
  • Photograph the stain, the source and any wet belongings.
  • Do not use a household vacuum or fans on sewage or contaminated water.

The drying clock

The U.S. EPA's guidance on mold says wet materials that are dried within 24 to 48 hours generally avoid mold growth. That window is a reason to call early and not wait to see whether a stain fades.

Our crews extract water, set air movers and dehumidifiers, and return to take readings until the materials test dry. If mold is already present, ask any mold company for its Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation license number and check it with the state. Our mold remediation page explains how that work is handled.

Extraction and drying in practice

Standing water goes first, using extraction units. Soaked carpet pad and wet insulation usually come out, and drywall that is soft or stained through is cut out. The rest is dried in place when the readings allow, which is why water mitigation starts before any rebuilding.

When the structure is dry, water damage repair replaces the drywall, texture and paint. For water that is still moving, emergency water removal is the right page.
