Water damage restoration for Silver Lake Estates homes

Silver Lake Estates is a single-family neighborhood in northeastern Grapevine, off Dove Loop Road between Main Street and Dooley Street. A Community Impact neighborhood profile from 2018 described roughly 350 homes, a build-out finished in 2007 and floor plans from about 2,000 to 4,750 square feet.

Those sizes matter when water gets loose. A supply line that fails in a utility room can send water under flooring into a hallway, a study and a kitchen before the first stain shows. Our crews map how far the moisture has traveled and then dry the structure, not just the visible puddle.

First hour: safety steps before a crew arrives

Keep every step safe and simple.

  • Stay out of any room where water is near outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, and call 911 or the electric utility for any shock or fire risk before calling us.
  • Shut off the water at the fixture valve or the main valve only if you can reach it without standing in water.
  • Keep children and pets out of the wet area, and treat water that came from a drain or toilet as contaminated.
  • Photograph the water, the source and the damaged items before anything is moved.
  • Lift small valuables and rugs to dry surfaces, and leave heavy furniture where it is.

What a drying job looks like in a larger home

After the source is stopped, extraction units pull standing water. Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera then show which walls, subfloors and cabinet bases are wet. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers are placed so air moves across every wet surface, and readings are taken again at each visit.

In a larger floor plan that can mean equipment in several rooms at once. Crews follow ANSI/IICRC S500, the published reference standard for professional water damage restoration, to decide what can be dried in place and what has to come out. Wet insulation and soaked carpet pad usually do not stay.

Causes we plan for inside homes like these

Most calls start inside the house, not outside it. Common sources are a failed washer hose, a leaking dishwasher line, a water heater that gives out and a toilet supply line that splits. Each has its own page, including appliance leak water damage and water heater leak cleanup.

When the water has already soaked a ceiling or wall, the next step is usually water damage repair after the structure is dry. If standing water is still spreading, emergency water removal comes first.

Flood maps and this neighborhood

To check a specific address, use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and ask your insurer or agent about flood coverage. Standard homeowners policies exclude flood damage, which needs a separate policy.

Insurance questions after a leak

Texas Department of Insurance guidance says most homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe or a broken washer hose, while gradual leaks and seepage are usually excluded. Your policy and your insurer decide what applies.

We photograph the loss, record moisture readings and keep an itemized scope you can share with your adjuster. We do not decide coverage and cannot promise how a claim will be handled.
