Lakeshore flooding in Flower Mound

Grapevine Lake forms much of Flower Mound's southern boundary, and Marshall Creek forms part of that same edge. The lake is a flood control reservoir on Denton Creek, so when heavy rain fills it, water moves toward neighborhoods on both sides.

In June 2015 the lake's spillway was used for the first time since 1981, FM 2499 between Grapevine and Flower Mound closed for an extended period, and Flower Mound signed an emergency declaration. The lake stayed above flood pool for more than 120 days that year, with parks and roads damaged.

Before help arrives: what to do if water keeps coming in

Take only the steps that are safe.

Stop the water

Close the fixture valve, the appliance valve or the main. If water is entering from outside, move to higher ground inside.

Treat the electrics with care

Never wade to the panel. Wet outlets or a wet panel mean stay out and call the electric utility or 911 first.

Lift contents

Raise valuables and paper onto counters or upper shelves.

Avoid contact

Lake, creek and drain water is contaminated. Keep children and pets away and wear boots and gloves if you must touch anything.

Capture the loss

Photograph the water line and the damage before moving anything.

Phone the 24/7 line

Tell the dispatcher how deep the water is and where it came from.

Neighborhood and landmark tie-backs

Flower Mound's Rock Ledge, Murrell and Twin Coves parks lie along the lake's North Shore trail, and The Mound itself, a 12.5-acre hill near FM 3040 and FM 2499, gave the town its name. Homes close to the lake or the creek should check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and ask their insurer about flood coverage.

The Lakeside Business District on the southern edge and the Denton Creek District on the western edge sit near water, and businesses there should confirm their own flood coverage separately from a homeowner's policy.

Water supply and growth

The Town of Flower Mound receives water from two wholesale sources, the Upper Trinity Regional Water District and Dallas Water Utilities, and provides wastewater service itself. The town was incorporated on February 25, 1961 and had 75,956 residents in 2020, up 17 percent from 2010. Growth followed the opening of Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport in 1974 and brought a wide mix of home ages. Newer homes see appliance and supply line failures, and older ones add aging roofs and plumbing.
