Burst Pipe in Grapevine, TX: The First Hour, Step by Step

Water is coming through a wall, a ceiling or a slab and every minute adds to the damage. Here is what to do, in order, before the crew arrives.

What to do when a pipe bursts in your Grapevine, TX home

Stop the water at its source, make the area electrically safe and start removing what has already spilled. That sequence matters more than anything else you do in the first hour. A split supply line can release hundreds of gallons in a short time, and the longer wet drywall, insulation and flooring stay saturated, the more of them end up in a dumpster.

Most homes have a main shutoff at the street meter box, a second valve where the line enters the house, and small angle stops under sinks, toilets and behind washers. Know which one you can reach fastest. Turn a main valve clockwise until it stops, and then open a faucet at the lowest point in the house to drain what is left in the lines.

Safety first: water and electricity

If water is dripping through a light fixture, pooling around outlets or reaching the electrical panel, stay out of that room. Call 911 or your electric utility before you call anyone else. Cut power at the breaker panel only if you can reach it while standing on dry ground, and never touch a switch with wet hands. If the ceiling is bowing or sagging with trapped water, keep everyone out from underneath it.

The first 60 minutes, in order

Work through these steps as quickly as safety allows. Each one limits how far the water travels.

  1. Shut off the main water valve, then open the lowest faucet to relieve pressure.
  2. Make the area safe. Switch off breakers serving wet rooms only if the panel is dry and reachable.
  3. Photograph and video the source, the wet surfaces and any damaged belongings before moving anything.
  4. Lift small valuables, books, shoes and electronics onto counters or into a dry room.
  5. Scoop or mop standing water, and use a wet vacuum on hard floors if the outlet it plugs into is dry and safe.
  6. Pull up rugs and pad, and prop up sofa and chair legs on foil or plastic to stop stains from wicking upward.
  7. Place a bucket under active drips, and poke a small hole in a bulging ceiling only if you can do it from a safe angle.
  8. Ask your insurer how to report the loss, and then call a restoration crew for extraction and drying.

Resist the urge to start repairs. A plumber needs to fix the broken section, but opened walls and removed flooring are decisions that follow a moisture reading, not a guess.

What the crew does after the pipe is fixed

A plumber stops the leak, and then restoration begins. Wet materials hold water long after the surface looks dry, so the work is measured rather than assumed.

Extraction and moisture mapping

Extraction units pull standing water from floors. A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera then trace the wet area through drywall, baseboards and subfloor, so the drying plan covers the full footprint instead of the visible stain.

Our mitigation service covers this first stage, including the decision about which materials can dry in place.

Drying and cleanup

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run for roughly 3 to 5 days in typical residential losses, with readings taken daily. Wet insulation and soaked drywall near the break are often removed so the cavity can dry. Details on that stage are in our cleanup guide, and any rebuild afterward falls under repair work.

Grapevine's mayor signed a disaster declaration on February 16, 2021, which let residents apply for FEMA assistance. Applicants had to report all of their insurance coverage and provide a settlement or denial letter, so keep every insurer letter together with your photos of a burst pipe loss.

Common mistakes in the first hour

The biggest one is waiting to see whether the leak stops by itself. Pressurized lines do not. The second is walking through the wet area with a household fan and no plan, which pushes humid air into rooms that were still dry.

People also throw away items before photographing them, which complicates a claim. And some homeowners tear out wet drywall in every room to be safe. Removing only what a moisture reading says is compromised keeps the rebuild smaller.

Insurance and the days that follow

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe, is often covered, while slow leaks and neglect often are not. Check your policy and ask your insurer what it requires, including how soon to report and which temporary repairs it expects.
